Why the 364 zodiac exists · the Ultracopernican idea

The classical zodiac was fixed about two thousand years ago. Since then the Earth's axis has slowly swung around (the precession of the equinoxes), so the twelve "signs" no longer match the constellations they were named after — and the sky is the only thing astrology can honestly hang on.

  • The 364 zodiac makes the real sky its origin: the year begins at the actual winter solstice, when the Sun reaches 0° Capricorn.
  • It divides the year into 13 signs × 28 days = 364 parts — 28 echoes the Moon's cycle and keeps the numbers human and round.
  • It brings back Ophiuchus, the 13th sign: the band of the zodiac in the sky really does contain thirteen constellations.
  • One zodiacal day is exactly 1/364 of the tropical year (≈ 1.0034 conventional days), so the year stays whole: geometry rules, time adapts — the clock bends, the sky never does.
  • Two honest meters: the calendar counts uniform 1/364 days, but the Sun’s position in the circle can run a fraction of a day ahead of or behind the clock (the equation of time). Real astronomy — the divergence is expected, not an error.

It is a credible alternative, not a replacement: classical astrology keeps its symbolic tradition, this one chooses instead to follow the moving, real sky.
